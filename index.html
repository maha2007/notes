Reading notes

Papers I've pulled apart

When a paper grabs me, I don't stop at the abstract. I go figure by figure, redraw the panels, and build small interactive versions of the ideas until the mechanism clicks.

Mostly neuroscience and brain–computer interfaces, with some protein and DNA mechanics. Each note keeps the questions I got stuck on along the way.

Language & thought

Four papers on where meaning and grammar live in the brain, read in this order.

  1. Huth et al. 2016Semantic maps that tile the cortexSeven people listen to two hours of stories in an fMRI scanner, and meaning turns out to be painted across both hemispheres in a shared ~140-area atlas.fMRIencoding modelssemantics
  2. Fedorenko, Ivanova & Regev 2024The language network as a natural kindFive left-hemisphere areas that do language and only language, sitting between perception below and reasoning above.fMRIlocalizerslanguage
  3. Frankland & Greene 2020In search of the brain's language of thoughtHow the brain builds brand-new thoughts from old parts: map-like codes in the default mode network, sentence-like role slots in temporal and prefrontal cortex.compositionalitygrid cellsDMN
  4. Cai et al. 2026Language neurons, one by one579 single neurons recorded while people chat: specialist cells for grammar, and an LLM that predicts them about a second before each word is spoken.single unitsLLMssyntax
