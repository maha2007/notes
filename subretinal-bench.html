Subretinal Pixel Bench

01

What the chain actually is

Where each stage sits, and what crosses each boundary

In dry AMD and retinitis pigmentosa the photoreceptors die but the bipolar and ganglion cells beneath them survive. Stimulate those survivors in the pattern light would have produced and the brain receives an image. The hard part is getting both power and image data into a chip under the retina with no wires through the eye wall.

PRIMA's answer is to make every pixel its own solar cell. The implant is entirely passive: photons land on a pixel, that pixel's own photodiodes convert them to current, and that current drives charge into the tissue directly underneath. Image and power arrive on the same beam. Infrared because the patient must not see it, and because far more optical power can cross the pupil safely at 880 nm than in the visible.

02

The physical bench

What sits where, and one deliberate departure from the real device

The real implant puts the photodiodes in the tissue. On a bench that would mean sealing semiconductors in saline on day one, which is a packaging project, not a physics project — and when a reading goes wrong you won't know whether the optics, the seal, or the electrochemistry failed.

So for Phase 1 and 2, keep the diodes dry. Illuminate them in air, and run two wires down into the cell to a pair of electrodes. Electrically it is the identical loop. You give up per-pixel locality, which you don't need until you build an array in Phase 3 — and by then you'll know what "working" looks like.

03

The circuit, and why two diodes

Including the part of the circuit that is made of saltwater

There is no power supply in this loop. The diodes are the source — that is the whole idea — which means the available voltage is fixed by silicon physics at roughly 0.5 V per junction, and the electrode–electrolyte interface needs closer to 0.8–1 V before meaningful charge moves. One diode will not do it. This is exactly why the real implant stacks two per pixel, and you should discover it on your own bench before you read it anywhere.

The interface itself is a circuit element, and it is the important one. Model it as a double-layer capacitance C_dl in parallel with a faradaic resistance R_f, in series with the solution resistance R_s. Charge that flows through C_dl is reversible and harmless. Charge that flows through R_f is a chemical reaction: it dissolves your electrode and damages tissue. Your entire safety argument is the ratio between them.

05

What the projector adds — and why not on day one

A DLP is the chip inside an ordinary office projector: a grid of microscopic aluminium mirrors, each tilting ±12° thousands of times a second. Tilt one way and light from the source goes out the lens; tilt the other and it goes into a beam dump. Grey levels come from the fraction of each frame a mirror spends "on". It is a spatial light modulator — it stamps an image onto whatever light you point at it, and nothing more.

A DLP evaluation board is that chip sold on a small PCB with its driver ASIC and an HDMI or USB input, so engineers can prototype with it. Your laptop sees it as a second monitor. Don't substitute a consumer projector: those carry an IR-cut filter in the light path by design, plus fixed frame timing and colour processing you cannot disable — all three of which break this experiment.

06

Build order

Fifteen steps, each with the reason it comes where it does

Phase 0 — calibrate before you measure  ·  ~1 weekend
01

Build the light-tight enclosure, and set your IR safety practice

A cardboard box lined with black felt, with a hand port. Get IR-blocking eyewear before the LED arrives, not after.

Why first880 nm is invisible, so your blink and aversion reflexes do not fire — the one genuine hazard here is dangerous precisely because it looks like nothing is happening. The enclosure earns its place twice: silicon photodiodes respond broadly across the near-IR, so room light and especially any incandescent source adds a large DC pedestal that swamps your signal.
02

Characterise the source: drive current → optical power → irradiance at the plane of the diode

Borrow a power meter from a lab for an afternoon. Record power at several drive currents, measure the illuminated spot area, and convert to mW/mm². Keep the LED on a fixed mount and never move it afterwards.

Why nowEvery result you produce is expressed per unit irradiance, and PRIMA's ceiling is a specific number — 3.5 mW/mm². Without an absolute calibration your dataset is a set of arbitrary units that cannot be compared to any published device, which is most of what makes the project worth showing.
Phase 1 — dry electrical  ·  ~1 weekend
03

Photodiode in the dark: I–V curve and dark current

With the enclosure closed, sweep and record. Confirm your amplifier's noise floor sits well below the currents you expect (tens of nA to a few µA).

WhyThis is your instrument baseline. If you skip it you will later spend a weekend chasing a "signal" that is your own amplifier's offset drift.
04

Photocurrent versus irradiance, into a known resistor

Sweep across the full irradiance range from step 2. Plot photocurrent against mW/mm² and check linearity.

Why before salineThis establishes the responsivity of your actual part in your actual geometry, and gives you a current budget. When the electrochemistry later behaves oddly you will know whether the light side is delivering what it should, because you measured it in isolation first.
05

Open-circuit voltage of one, two and three diodes in series

Measure V_oc for each configuration, still dry.

Why this is the good stepYou will find roughly 0.5 V per junction, largely independent of how hard you drive the light — because V_oc depends only logarithmically on illumination. That is the constraint that forces PRIMA to stack diodes. Seeing it on the bench, and being able to say why more light does not fix it, is the most instructive result of Phase 1.
Phase 2 — wet electrochemistry, the core of the project  ·  ~3–4 weekends
06

Assemble the three-electrode cell

PBS at pH 7.4. A small platinum active electrode of accurately known geometric area, a platinum mesh return electrode with area at least fifty times larger, and an Ag/AgCl reference. Chloridise your own silver wire if buying a reference is out of budget.

Why the size asymmetryCurrent density is what drives electrochemistry, so making the return electrode much larger keeps its interface far from any interesting regime. All the chemistry then happens at the one electrode whose area you know, and every µC/cm² you report belongs to that electrode alone.
07

Baseline the electrode electrochemically, before any light

Run electrochemical impedance spectroscopy across roughly 1 Hz–100 kHz, and a few cyclic voltammetry cycles inside the water window. Extract R_s and C_dl.

Why you need these two numbersThey are the parameters of Figure 3. You cannot decompose a transient into access voltage and polarization without knowing R_s, and you cannot say whether the charge you delivered was capacitive without knowing what C_dl should have absorbed. CV also verifies the electrode is clean and that its electrochemically active area matches its geometric area — those two differ by a surprising factor on rough platinum.
08

First light-driven pulse into saline

Single 5 ms pulse. Capture Ch 1 and Ch 2 simultaneously. Save the raw trace.

WhyThis is the first real datapoint of the project and the moment the whole chain is proven end to end. Archive the raw waveform, not a processed number — you will reinterpret it three times as your understanding improves.
09

Sweep irradiance and pulse width; build the charge-density dataset

Integrate Ch 1 over each pulse, divide by the active area, and tabulate µC/cm² against irradiance and duration. Automate the sweep and the integration.

Why automateThe parameter space is two-dimensional and each point needs a settling interval, so this is hours of repetitive triggering and it is exactly the kind of measurement loop you already know how to script. It is also where a hand-run experiment quietly accumulates inconsistency.
10

Decompose every transient using Figure 4

For each pulse, extract access voltage, polarization slope, and residual offset after decay. Flag any pulse whose ramp deviates from linear.

WhyCharge density alone is not a safety claim. This step is what converts a number into an argument — it tells you which fraction of the delivered charge was reversible, which is the only version of the number a device engineer will accept.
11

Find your electrode's ceiling empirically

Raise charge density until the transient shows faradaic onset or the electrode potential leaves the water window (roughly −0.6 to +0.8 V vs Ag/AgCl on platinum). Record the threshold. Inspect the electrode afterwards.

Why measure rather than citeThe often-quoted limit for smooth platinum is around 0.35 µC/cm², but that figure is geometry- and waveform-dependent and your electrode is neither smooth nor identical to the one it came from. A measured ceiling on your own electrode is a result. A cited one is a footnote.
12

Upgrade the coating and repeat steps 7–11

Electrodeposit PEDOT:PSS, or grow iridium oxide, onto an identical electrode. Rerun the full characterisation.

Why this is the strongest single resultCoatings raise the injectable charge ceiling by increasing effective surface area and adding reversible faradaic capacity — often by an order of magnitude. Showing the ceiling move on your own bench, with the before-and-after EIS to explain it, demonstrates you understand the actual materials lever every implant company pulls.
Phase 3 — patterning, arrays, and the result  ·  ~3–4 weekends
13

Add the DMD; build a small array; measure crosstalk

Illuminate one pixel and measure the current at its neighbours. Report crosstalk as a fraction, against pitch and against the optical spot size.

Why crosstalk and not resolutionAn array of passive photovoltaic pixels sharing an electrolyte is not the same thing as N independent pixels — current spreads through the solution, and neighbouring return paths couple. Crosstalk, not mirror count, is what actually limits achievable contrast, and measuring it is the step that turns "I built a projector" into "I characterised an array".
14

Shrink the effective pixel and repeat

Mask the diode area down and pair it with proportionally smaller electrodes, at several sizes. Rerun steps 9–11 at each. This produces the horizontal axis of Figure 6.

Why this is the whole pointCharge per pixel falls with collecting area while the charge density needed to reach the target neurons rises as the pixel gets small relative to its distance from them. Those two trends close the operating window at some pitch, and that pitch is the acuity limit of the entire technology. Producing that crossing point from your own measurements is the headline result.
15

Close the loop: camera → encoder → projector at 30 Hz

Contrast enhancement, edge emphasis, mapping to your available grey levels, driving the DMD. Report end-to-end latency and where it is spent.

Why lastIt is the least uncertain part of the project and the part most likely to expand to fill available time. Done at the end it completes the systems story; done at the start it eats the weekends the electrochemistry needed.

09

Failure modes, named in advance

Each of these will cost you a weekend if you meet it without expecting it

Safety

Invisible beam, no reflex

At 880 nm your eye gives you no warning at all. Milliwatt LEDs are manageable; a laser diode at any real power is not something to align casually. Eyewear on, beam below eye level, enclosure closed.

Expected

One diode won't drive the interface

Roughly 0.5 V per junction, and more light barely helps. Plan for two in series from the start, and record the single-diode result anyway — it is a finding, not a false start.

Silent

Reference electrode drift

Any appreciable current through the reference destroys its potential. Buffer it with a genuinely high-impedance input, and re-check it against a second reference weekly.

Silent

Uncontrolled electrode area

A wire dipped into solution has whatever area the meniscus decides. Epoxy or heat-shrink everything but a defined tip, and state how you determined the area.

Silent

Stray IR pedestal

Silicon photodiodes see far more than you do. Any incandescent lamp, sunlight, or an IR remote in the room adds a DC offset. Work dark, or modulate and detect at the modulation frequency.

Procurement

IR-cut filters everywhere

Consumer projectors, most webcams and many lenses are deliberately blind at 880 nm. Check transmission at your wavelength before buying any optic.

Data

Dissolved oxygen

Oxygen reduction is a faradaic path that will show up in your transients and shift your apparent ceiling. Either purge with nitrogen or report that you did not, consistently.

Data

Sense resistor too large

You only have about a volt in the whole loop. A large sense resistor buys you signal and steals the voltage the interface needed. Pick it, then verify the loop still delivers the charge you expect.

10

What to publish

Write it as a short methods paper, not a build log. Four things carry it:

  • Figure 6 with your own numbers on the axes — the crossing point, and the pitch at which your window closes.
  • A before-and-after ceiling from step 12 — bare platinum versus coated, with the EIS that explains the difference.
  • One transient, fully decomposed — access voltage, polarization, residual — showing you can tell reversible charge from damage.
  • The failure mode you hit and did not expect. Every real bench has one. Including it is what makes a reader believe the rest of the numbers.

Then state plainly what the bench cannot tell you: there are no neurons in it. You have measured the electrical and electrochemical half of the problem and bounded the space a real device must live in — which is a precise and defensible claim, and a far better one than an overreach about restoring vision.

Scope discipline

Phase 0–2 alone is a complete, defensible project with a real result. If the DMD never arrives, or the array never works, stop at step 12 and write up what you have. A finished small project beats an abandoned large one every time, and the electrochemistry is the half that is hard to fake.
